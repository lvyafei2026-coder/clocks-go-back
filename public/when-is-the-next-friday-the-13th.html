When Is the Next Friday the 13th?

Friday the 13th has no fixed slot on the calendar the way New Year's Day does. It appears whenever the 13th of a month lands on a Friday, which depends entirely on the weekday that month began on. Once you know that rule, you can find the next date on any calendar in under a minute — and understand why the gaps between them are so uneven.

The Rule That Decides Every Friday the 13th

There is no fixed date for Friday the 13th. It shows up whenever the 13th day of a month happens to fall on a Friday, so it moves around from year to year and can land in almost any month. The reason is plain arithmetic: the weekday of the 13th is decided by the weekday of the 1st. Move twelve days forward from the start of a month and you arrive on the same weekday shifted by five.

Twelve days is five days past a full week, so a month that opens on a Sunday has its 13th on a Friday. Open on a Monday and the 13th is a Saturday; open on a Tuesday and it is a Sunday, and so on through the week. That single rule settles every case, which is why you never need a table to work out whether a given month qualifies.

How to Find the Next Date

To find the next Friday the 13th, check the 1st of the current month on a calendar, then the months that follow. The first month whose 1st falls on a Sunday gives you the next date. The search is always short: no gap between two Friday the 13ths runs longer than fourteen months, so looking ahead a year and a bit is enough.

The wait itself varies a lot. Sometimes the next one is only weeks away — February and March line up back to back when a common-year February begins on a Sunday. Other times you go more than a year between them. That uneven rhythm is why the date feels random even though it follows strict rules.

How Many Friday the 13ths in a Year

Every year in the Gregorian calendar contains at least one Friday the 13th. Most years have one or two, and a few have three, which is the maximum possible. Three requires a particular arrangement of short and long months: February, March and November together form the best-known combination.

Over long spans, the 13th is slightly more likely to be a Friday than any other weekday. Month lengths and the leap-year rule spread weekdays unevenly across the calendar, and Friday edges ahead by a small margin. The difference is tiny, but it comes from the structure of the calendar rather than from superstition.

Why the Dates Repeat 28 Years Later

The calendar's weekday pattern repeats when the same mix of common and leap years returns in the same order. In most stretches that takes 28 years: seven possible starting weekdays multiplied by four leap-year positions. A Friday the 13th in a given month will often fall on the same date 28 years later.

The repetition is not permanent. Years divisible by 100 skip the leap day unless they are also divisible by 400, so 2100 will break the cycle that held through the 1900s and 2000s. Across those century boundaries the pattern shifts, and a date can return after 12, 40 or another number of years instead.

February and the Back-to-Back Case

February is the month that produces the tightest clusters. In a common year, February has exactly 28 days, which is four full weeks, so the weekday of 1 March is identical to the weekday of 1 February. If February starts on a Sunday, March does too — and both months deliver a Friday the 13th four weeks apart.

Add a leap day and that link breaks. A 29-day February pushes March's start forward by one weekday, so the two months no longer match. This is why back-to-back Friday the 13ths in February and March appear only in certain years and never in the same rhythm twice running.

FAQ

How often is there a Friday the 13th?

At least once every calendar year. Two in a year is common, and three is possible but less frequent — three is the maximum.

What is the longest gap between Friday the 13ths?

Fourteen months. No stretch without one runs longer than that, whatever the mix of common and leap years.

Can February 13 fall on a Friday?

Yes. It happens in common years where February begins on a Sunday, and in that case 13 March is a Friday too, four weeks later.

Do Friday the 13ths repeat every 28 years?

Usually, because the weekday pattern of the calendar returns after 28 years in most spans. Century years that skip the leap day, such as 2100, interrupt the cycle.

Why is the 13th more often a Friday than other days?

Because month lengths and the leap-year rule distribute weekdays unevenly, Friday lands on the 13th slightly more often than any other weekday over the full calendar cycle.
